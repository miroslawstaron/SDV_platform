Imagery prepared every 25 metres

0 m25 m50 m75 m100 m
  1. Sample the prepared route at fixed driving-distance points.
  2. Find suitable nearby imagery and preload image bytes in browser memory.
  3. Render the current image and the next two views in three buffers.
  4. Switch the visible buffer when the car crosses the next 25 m boundary.

Position and heading remain live between switches. A missing or unprepared image shows the vehicle on the map.

The route is clipped to its snapped endpoints. The 371 m demo has 15 image stations from 0 through 350 metres. Searches are capped at one per second with two jobs in flight; image bytes use a bounded 64 MiB cache. Future preloads and rendered buffers stay in memory. Source images actually displayed during live driving are archived separately with their pHashes. Source: web/src/imageryRoute.ts, imageryPreload.ts, memoryCache.ts and mapillaryViewer.ts.

Captured images and pHash matching

Capture what you reach

Keep the driver’s view open. Each displayed 25 m source image is saved with its 64-bit perceptual hash.

Image bytes, provider ID, location and capture date persist in the Docker data volume.

Compare against the database

A match differs by at most 8 of 64 pHash bits.

Closest similarity = 100 × (1 − differing bits / 64).

Only the current capture is excluded. Earlier captures of the same image match at 100%; image bytes are stored once.

Panoramas use the full source image. Future preloads and replay do not add captures.

Captures are stored in /data/images.sqlite separately from vehicle recordings. Capture observations are unique by run and 25 m station; identical bytes are deduplicated by SHA-256. pHash uses a grayscale 32×32 resize and the first 8×8 DCT coefficients, producing 64 bits. Matching counts other run/station captures, including earlier captures of the same stored image. Duplicate uploads for one run/station do not increase the count. Source: backend/sdv/imagery.py, backend/sdv/api.py, web/src/Driver.tsx and web/src/mapillaryViewer.ts.

How image similarity is measured

Similarity = 100 × (1 − d / 64), where d is the number of differing pHash bits.

Differing bits (of 64)SimilarityComparison
0100.0%Identical pHash
198.4%Similar capture
887.5%Match threshold
1675.0%Below threshold

A match needs d ≤ 8. A 100% score means identical hashes; “Same image” also confirms identical stored bytes.

Hamming distance is the bit count of the XOR of the two 64-bit pHashes. The dashboard reports the largest similarity among other captures, rounded to one decimal place. The exact score for d=1 is 98.4375%, displayed as 98.4%. A d=9 comparison scores 85.9375% (85.9%) and is below the match threshold. pHash equality alone does not prove byte equality: the gallery’s Same image label compares SHA-256 image IDs. This is a hash resemblance measure, not geographic distance or a confidence score. Source: backend/sdv/imagery.py (HASH_BITS, MAX_DISTANCE and similarity), web/src/imageSimilarity.ts and web/src/SimilarityDashboard.tsx.

Ground truth and reported signals

Ground truth

SUMO supplies actual position, speed, battery energy and journey progress.

The map, imagery and instrument panel use these values.

ECU reports

Sensor and BMS messages pass through faultable transport adapters.

The engineering test bench compares these messages against actual vehicle state.

A corrupted sensor can mislead the controller while the visualized vehicle stays at its true position.

Ground truth is recorded separately from decoded network signals. The instrument display is intentionally an observer; it does not emulate an infotainment ECU consuming only faulty CAN. Source: backend simulator and worker, web/src/Dashboard.tsx, instruments/public/dashboard.js.

Electric energy affects the drive

Consumption and regeneration
change battery energy.

SUMO's simplified electric model includes vehicle mass, drag, rolling losses and conversion efficiency.

Below 10%
BMS issues a low charge warning
From 5% to empty
Permitted propulsion decreases linearly
True depletion
The plant prevents positive propulsion

Ground truth depletion still applies when reported battery values are corrupted. Thermal and electrochemical behavior are outside this model.

Energy model parameters are pinned in backend/dependencies.json and provenance. The instrument power scale is an illustrative display scale, not a new actuator limit. Source: backend/sdv/simulator.py, controls.py and application.py.

Preparing another route

  1. Click start and destination on the engineering map.
  2. Download a bounded OSM region and convert it to a SUMO road network.
  3. Snap both endpoints to passenger-car lanes within 100 metres.
  4. Calculate and validate the route against that same network.
  5. Review adjusted endpoints, then start a 100 m–5 km journey.

Preparation has progress, cancellation, bounded expansion and caching. Uncached roads and Mapillary require internet.

The initial margin is one kilometre, expandable up to twice with maximum area 100 square kilometres. Right hand traffic is the default; left hand preparation is available. Disconnected roads or invalid lengths are rejected. Source: backend/sdv/maps.py and README.md.

Complete recordings and exports

Record separately

Vehicle state, CAN transmit and receive, SOME/IP events, faults, run configuration and results.

Recorder loss explicitly invalidates the run.

Export and review

JSONL: full recording and configuration.

Events CSV: raw messages and decoded payloads.

Signals CSV: one row per signal observation, including source, destination, time, unit and VSS mapping.

The live trace is sampled. Exports read the complete vehicle recording. Captured images and pHashes use a separate persistent database; tokens are excluded.

Exports during an active run are consistent snapshots; export again at completion for the full journey. Duplicate and receive events are not collapsed. Recordings persist in the Docker volume until explicitly deleted. Source: backend/sdv/storage.py and recorder.py; README.md.

Replay and replaceable modules

Replay the same evidence

Pause, play and scrub a recorded run. Vehicle state, signals, sequence diagram, instruments and imagery follow one cursor.

Rerun the saved configuration to create a new experiment.

Change one module

Keep its message contracts and replace its container image or application command.

Python and C++ controllers, a gRPC adapter API, DBC payloads and VSS mappings are supplied.

The simulator backend interface isolates SUMO, with a later CARLA implementation reserved for 3D steering dynamics.

CONTROLLER_COMMAND is available in .env and Compose overrides can swap images. Existing examples limit acceleration to 1 m/s². A future backend is a design boundary, not an implemented CARLA feature. Source: examples, sdk, docs/extensions.md and contracts.

Run your first experiment

  1. Launch with scripts/Start-Sdv.ps1 and open localhost:8080.
  2. Select the Gothenburg demonstration and start the drive.
  3. Open the driver's view; watch instruments beside the map and imagery.
  4. Inspect E/E architecture and the actual communication sequence.
  5. Drop a command or corrupt a sensor, then replay and export the run.

Set MAPILLARY_ACCESS_TOKEN for street imagery and keep the driver's view open for captures. Hover over UI elements for 3 seconds to see an explanation.

The launcher builds local images, starts Docker Compose and records dependency provenance. The README explains acquiring a Mapillary client token and restarting configuration. Do not publish the token in slides, logs or recordings. Source: scripts/Start-Sdv.ps1 and README.md.

Model boundaries and reference code

What this platform represents

Functional CAN delivery, real SOME/IP services and a simplified road and electric vehicle model.

Mapillary photos are sampled scenery. They do not depict simulated traffic, obstacles or traffic signal states.
