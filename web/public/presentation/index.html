Inside a software-defined vehicle

A virtual electric car with control software you can inspect and replace

Measure. Understand. Improve.

This platform runs locally with Docker Desktop on Windows. SUMO is the current road simulator. The ECUs exchange real functional CAN and SOME/IP messages, and their decisions change the simulated drive. Source: compose.yaml and README.md.

An electronic control unit (ECU) is a small software module with a vehicle task, such as braking or reporting speed.

The demonstration uses genuine OpenStreetMap roads and works offline. Online route preparation accepts 100 metres to 5 kilometres. Dimensions are 4.5 by 1.8 metres, mass 1,800 kg. Source: backend fixtures, configuration and simulator; README.md.

The services behind the dashboards

Each Docker container runs a service in its own environment. The detail slides explain all 16 services and their software sources.

How the control units communicate

Navigation → controller
→ drive, brake and lane units
→ simulator → sensors

The controller decides what to do. The actuator units apply its requests; sensors report the result.

SOME/IP · network services
Navigation sends the desired speed, lane and distance left.
CAN FD · vehicle messages
Control requests, applied actions and sensor feedback.
VSS · standard signal names
KUKSA stores reported values for other software to read.

Click a control unit or message in E/E architecture to see who sends it, who uses it and its current values.

Navigation also observes CAN through its adapter; its intent uses SOME/IP. A control command must traverse the controller and the actuator ECU before plant propulsion changes. Source: contracts/catalog.json, contracts/vehicle.dbc, web/src/ArchitectureView.tsx, backend/sdv/application.py.

Imagery prepared every 25 metres

0 m25 m50 m75 m100 m
  1. Mark a point every 25 metres along the prepared route.
  2. Find a suitable nearby street photo for each point.
  3. Load photos ahead of time so the next view is ready.
  4. Switch photos when the car reaches the next point.

The car’s position keeps updating between photo changes. If a photo is unavailable, the map still shows the drive.

The route is clipped to its snapped endpoints. The 371 m demo has 15 image stations from 0 through 350 metres. Searches are capped at one per second with two jobs in flight; image bytes use a bounded 64 MiB cache. Future preloads and rendered buffers stay in memory. Source images actually displayed during live driving are archived separately with their pHashes. Source: web/src/imageryRoute.ts, imageryPreload.ts, memoryCache.ts and mapillaryViewer.ts.

Captured images and pHash matching

Save the views you reach

Keep the driver view open. Each displayed street image is saved at its 25 m point.

Its pHash is a 64-bit visual fingerprint: a compact description of the image’s appearance.

The photo, fingerprint, source date and location remain in the database after the drive.

Find earlier similar views

The platform compares that fingerprint with earlier captures from every drive.

A view counts as similar when at most 8 of its 64 bits differ: a score of at least 87.5%.

The current capture is excluded. Seeing the same photo on another drive can produce a 100% match.

For a panorama, the full source photo is hashed. Loading future views or replaying a recording does not add captures.

Captures are stored in /data/images.sqlite separately from vehicle recordings. Capture observations are unique by run and 25 m station; identical bytes are deduplicated by SHA-256. pHash uses a grayscale 32×32 resize and the first 8×8 DCT coefficients, producing 64 bits. Matching counts other run/station captures, including earlier captures of the same stored image. Duplicate uploads for one run/station do not increase the count. Source: backend/sdv/imagery.py, backend/sdv/api.py, web/src/Driver.tsx and web/src/mapillaryViewer.ts.

How image similarity is measured

Compare the 64 bits in two visual fingerprints. Fewer differences mean a higher score:

Similarity = 100 × (1 − differing bits / 64)

Differing bitsSimilarityInterpretation
0100.0%Identical fingerprints
198.4%Counts as a similar view
887.5%Lowest score that counts as a match
1675.0%Does not count as a match

100% means the fingerprints match. The gallery’s “Same image” label also confirms identical photo files.

Hamming distance is the bit count of the XOR of the two 64-bit pHashes. The dashboard reports the largest similarity among other captures, rounded to one decimal place. The exact score for d=1 is 98.4375%, displayed as 98.4%. A d=9 comparison scores 85.9375% (85.9%) and is below the match threshold. pHash equality alone does not prove byte equality: the gallery’s Same image label compares SHA-256 image IDs. This is a hash resemblance measure, not geographic distance or a confidence score. Source: backend/sdv/imagery.py (HASH_BITS, MAX_DISTANCE and similarity), web/src/imageSimilarity.ts and web/src/SimilarityDashboard.tsx.

Ground truth and reported signals

Ground truth: what the car is doing

The simulator calculates the car’s actual position, speed, battery energy and journey progress.

The map and driver instruments use these values.

Reports: what the controller hears

The sensor and battery units send messages describing the car’s state.

Fault tests can change or delay those messages. The engineering dashboard shows the difference.

For example, a sensor can report 288 km/h while the car is moving much more slowly. The controller sees the false report; the instruments show actual speed.

Ground truth is recorded separately from decoded network signals. The instrument display is intentionally an observer; it does not emulate an infotainment ECU consuming only faulty CAN. Source: backend simulator and worker, web/src/Dashboard.tsx, instruments/public/dashboard.js.

Watchdogs: what happens when updates stop?

Missing or old informationResponse from a working control unit
Sensor or navigation update is over 200 ms oldController requests braking and zero acceleration
Drive command is over 200 ms oldPowertrain removes drive power
Brake command is over 200 ms oldBrake unit requests normal braking
Brake application itself has failedThe braking request may never reach the simulator

A watchdog checks for missing updates. Verify the car actually slowed down, as well as checking that a stop was requested.

Reference behavior from backend/sdv/application.py and backend/tests/test_live.py. Default normal deceleration is 3 m/s². Stopping is evaluated from ground-truth state, not merely the presence of a stop command. A failed brake application is different from missing controller commands at a working brake application.

Electric energy affects the drive

Driving uses energy.
Braking can recover it.

The simplified battery model accounts for vehicle mass, air resistance, rolling losses and conversion efficiency.

Below 10% charge
The battery unit shows a low-charge warning.
Below 5% charge
Available drive power falls as the battery empties.
At empty
The simulator prevents further acceleration.

Actual depletion still limits the car if battery reports are faulty. Battery temperature and internal chemistry are outside this model.

Energy model parameters are pinned in backend/dependencies.json and provenance. The instrument power scale is an illustrative display scale, not a new actuator limit. Source: backend/sdv/simulator.py, controls.py and application.py.

Research idea: how reliable are image matches?

What to try

  • Have people label pairs as the same view, related views or different scenes.
  • Try pHash match limits from 0 to 16 differing bits.
  • Compare pHash with DINOv2, a learned image-feature method.

What to measure

  • How many reported matches are correct, and how many repeats are missed.
  • Errors when lighting, cropping or viewing direction changes.
  • Processing time and memory needed for each image.

Proposed study · Baseline: current 64-bit pHash · Reference: DINOv2 paper.

Proposed research, not an implemented feature. Existing inputs: saved images, source metadata and pHashes. DINOv2 provides general visual representations; whether they improve repetition detection here must be tested, not assumed. Primary reference: Oquab et al., DINOv2: Learning Robust Visual Features without Supervision, https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07193. Local baseline: backend/sdv/imagery.py and image capture database.
